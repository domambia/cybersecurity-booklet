# Appendix B · Annotated Bibliography

> **How to use this:** You do not read all of these. Pick the one or two for your current chapter/track, work through them, and move on. "Foundational" books reward re-reading across your career; "hands-on" books are worked through with a lab; "reference" books are consulted, not read cover-to-cover. Free resources are marked **[free]**. Prices and editions change — verify currency.

---

## Learning how to learn (do these first — Chapter 1)
- **Barbara Oakley — *A Mind for Numbers* / *Learning How to Learn* (Coursera) [free course].** How technical learning actually works. Pays for itself immediately.
- **Brown, Roediger, McDaniel — *Make It Stick*.** The evidence for testing and spaced repetition over re-reading.
- **Austin Kleon — *Show Your Work!*** The short case for learning in public (Chapter 32).

## Foundational — the field's classics (revisit across your career)
- **Ross Anderson — *Security Engineering* (3rd ed.) [free PDF].** The definitive text on how security systems really work and fail. Dense; return to it repeatedly. (Chapters 2, 10.)
- **Cliff Stoll — *The Cuckoo's Egg*.** A true hacking-investigation story that teaches the mindset. Read for enjoyment early. (Chapter 2.)
- **Bruce Schneier — *Secrets and Lies* / *Applied Cryptography* (historical).** On security thinking and (the latter) crypto history.
- **Kim Zetter — *Countdown to Zero Day*.** The Stuxnet story; nation-state attacks made vivid.
- **Andy Greenberg — *Sandworm*.** Real APT campaigns and their consequences. Reads like a thriller.

## Computing & networking foundations (Chapters 4, 7)
- **Bryant & O'Hallaron — *Computer Systems: A Programmer's Perspective (CSAPP)*.** The operational understanding of memory, processes, execution. Chapters 3 & 9 especially.
- **Jon Erickson — *Hacking: The Art of Exploitation* (2nd ed.).** The best bridge from CS grad to understanding exploits; bundled Linux env. (Chapter 4.)
- **Kurosen & Ross — *Computer Networking: A Top-Down Approach*.** The standard networking text. **[free]** Charles Kozierok — *The TCP/IP Guide* (online).
- **Chris Sanders — *Practical Packet Analysis*.** Hands-on Wireshark. (Chapter 7.)

## Operating systems (Chapters 5, 6)
- **William Shotts — *The Linux Command Line* [free at linuxcommand.org].** Best free Linux shell book. (Chapter 5.)
- **OccupyTheWeb — *Linux Basics for Hackers*.** Linux specifically for security, using Kali.
- **Russinovich et al. — *Windows Internals*.** The deep Windows reference. Consult as needed. (Chapter 6.)
- **[free] adsecurity.org & thehacker.recipes.** The best free Active Directory attack/defense material. (Chapters 6, 18.)

## Scripting (Chapter 8)
- **Al Sweigart — *Automate the Boring Stuff with Python* [free].** Practical Python ramp.
- **TJ O'Connor — *Violent Python*** and **Justin Seitz — *Black Hat Python* (2nd ed.).** Python for security tooling.

## Security core (Chapters 10–13)
- **Adam Shostack — *Threat Modeling: Designing for Security*.** The definitive threat-modeling book (STRIDE, the four questions). (Chapter 10.)
- **Jean-Philippe Aumasson — *Serious Cryptography* (2nd ed.).** The best applied-crypto book for practitioners. (Chapter 11.)
- **[free] Cryptopals (cryptopals.com).** Learn crypto by breaking it. Do Sets 1–2. (Chapter 11.)
- **[free] MITRE ATT&CK (attack.mitre.org) & "Getting Started with ATT&CK".** The field's index. (Chapter 13.)

## Offensive security (Chapters 14–19)
- **Georgia Weidman — *Penetration Testing: A Hands-On Introduction to Hacking*.** Excellent whole-methodology primer with a lab. (Part 3.)
- **[free] PortSwigger Web Security Academy.** World-class free web-hacking training. **The** resource for Chapter 16.
- **Stuttard & Pinto — *The Web Application Hacker's Handbook*.** The classic comprehensive web text.
- **Michael Bazzell — *Open Source Intelligence Techniques*.** The OSINT reference. (Chapter 14.)
- **[free] HackTricks (book.hacktricks.xyz), GTFOBins, LOLBAS, PayloadsAllTheThings.** Practical offensive references.
- **[free] Nmap Network Scanning (nmap.org/book) — Fyodor.** (Chapter 15.)
- **TCM Security — *Practical Ethical Hacking* / *Privilege Escalation* courses.** Affordable, hands-on. (Chapters 14–17.)

## Defensive security (Chapters 20–25)
- **Don Murdoch — *Blue Team Handbook* (Incident Response Edition).** Practical field guide. (Chapter 22.)
- **[free] NIST SP 800-61.** The IR lifecycle authority. (Chapter 22.)
- **Ligh, Case, Levy, Walters — *The Art of Memory Forensics*.** Definitive memory forensics. (Chapter 23.)
- **Harlan Carvey — *Windows Forensic Analysis*.** Essential Windows artifact detail. (Chapter 23.)
- **Sikorski & Honig — *Practical Malware Analysis*.** The malware-analysis book, with a lab. (Chapter 24.)
- **Roberts & Brown — *Intelligence-Driven Incident Response*.** Ties CTI and IR. (Chapter 25.)
- **[free] SigmaHQ, Atomic Red Team, The DFIR Report, Splunk BOTS.** Detection, validation, and realistic data. (Chapters 20–21, 25.)
- **[free] SANS DFIR posters & cheat sheets.** Print the "Hunt Evil" and "Windows Forensic Analysis" posters.

## Modern platforms (Chapters 26–29)
- **[free] flaws.cloud & flaws2.cloud (Scott Piper).** Brilliant hands-on AWS security. (Chapter 26.)
- **Liz Rice — *Container Security*.** The definitive container-isolation book. (Chapter 27.)
- **Martin & Hausenblas — *Hacking Kubernetes*.** K8s offense and defense. (Chapter 27.)
- **Julien Vehent — *Securing DevOps*.** DevSecOps philosophy and practice. (Chapter 28.)
- **[free] OWASP LLM Top 10 (2026) & Agentic Top 10 (genai.owasp.org); MITRE ATLAS (atlas.mitre.org); NIST AI RMF.** The AI-security syllabus. (Chapter 29.)
- **[free] Simon Willison's blog.** The best ongoing writing on prompt injection / LLM security. (Chapter 29.)

## GRC, privacy, career (Chapters 30–36)
- **[free] NIST CSF 2.0 & CIS Controls v8.** The organizing framework and the actionable controls. (Chapter 30.)
- **CISSP Official Study Guide (Chapple et al.).** Excellent GRC/management education even if you don't sit the exam. (Chapter 30.)
- **[free] GDPR text (gdpr-info.eu) & your national DPA's guides (e.g., ICO).** Privacy obligations. (Chapter 31.)
- **Michael Watkins — *The First 90 Days*.** Starting a role well. (Chapter 35.)
- **Anders Ericsson — *Peak*.** How expertise is actually built (deliberate practice). (Chapter 36.)
- **Cal Newport — *So Good They Can't Ignore You* / *Deep Work*.** Career strategy and focus. (Chapters 1, 36.)

## Practice platforms (use continuously)
- **[free/low-cost] TryHackMe** (guided, beginner→intermediate), **Hack The Box / HTB Academy** (challenge-based), **PortSwigger Academy [free]** (web), **OverTheWire [free]** (Linux/binary), **picoCTF [free]** (breadth), **LetsDefend / CyberDefenders / Blue Team Labs Online** (defensive/DFIR), **VulnHub [free]** (target VMs).

## Staying current (curate a few)
- **The DFIR Report, Red Canary Threat Detection Report, Verizon DBIR** — how attacks actually happen.
- **Newsletters/podcasts:** pick 2–3 (e.g., tl;dr sec, Risky Business, Darknet Diaries) — signal over noise (Chapter 36).
- **Conference talks:** DEF CON, Black Hat, BSides — free on YouTube.
